* Source checks that explain the optional cache boundary
S: [[file:../../priv/lib/datom.bl::(defn memo-by-basis][memo-by-basis]] identifies a DB view and calls the bounded cache; no cache store
identity falls straight through to computation. Its docstring/comment says
"computed once" although the actual call delegates to an optional cache.
S: [[file:../../priv/lib/data/bounded-cache.bl::(defn value][value]] looks up a key/revision, calls =f= on a miss, calls =put!= and returns
=v= regardless of publication success. Lookup/put failures are conservative
misses/false, computation exceptions propagate, and one revision occupies each
logical key. Concurrent misses can each compute; this is not single-flight.
The revision adds callback module/new_uniq/index identity, not a complete semantic
proof of its captured environment or transitive dependency validity. The caller
must still satisfy the pure =db/key= contract and identity/freshness requirements.

S: [[file:../../priv/lib/data/bounded-cache.bl::(defn footprint][footprint]] charges conservative flattened term size, external size, retained
sub-binary parents, owner/ETS overhead and a factor of two. Function/native-resource
or too-deep values decline rather than becoming free entries. The charge is not
RSS or a precise physical heap graph. Repeated references can cost more under
this accounting even if locally shared; sharing may change across actor/ETS
boundaries. Removing =:of= is consistent with the supplied measured charge drop;
it is not evidence that 6.25MB of actual process RAM was freed.
S: [[file:../../priv/lib/data/cache-budget.bl][budget]] defines MAX-ENTRY=8MiB, MAX-BYTES=32MiB and finite entry count.

* Cache admission needs four independent questions
1. Is reuse semantically valid? Bind DB view/read dependencies, schema, policy,
   provider catalogue, actual code/generation and all non-DB inputs. Computed
   shadows or unknown dependencies refuse reuse unless independently covered.
2. Is the value retainable? Finite first-order data, supported key identity,
   conservative charge below entry limit, bounded aggregate/storage lifetime.
3. Was it actually retained/reused? Reserve/publication outcome, current matching
   lookup, eviction/owner/generation state. Preflight success is not a hit.
4. Is missing the cache operationally admissible? Computation, normalization,
   charging, copies, concurrent duplicate work and response construction have
   budgets independently of retention. A miss must not bypass authorization.

A proposed diagnostic receipt could carry separate =validity=, =admission=,
=publication=, =lookup= and =fallback-work= fields, with exact input/model/code
identity and safe reasons such as oversized, opaque, stale, unknown-provider,
busy, evicted or revision-mismatch. These are design fields, not existing API
statuses. Current false/miss results often do not expose the cause; report unknown
reason instead of fabricating a precise one. Diagnostics must be bounded and
redact raw world/closure contents. Availability or a receipt shape never grants
read/write authority. A failed proof can trigger a separately authorized bounded
computation; it cannot authorize an unsafe raw fallback.

Do not "solve" this case by increasing cache limits or weakening resource checks.
First separate canonical data from ephemeral local acceleration, observe the
charge and result, and bound the miss path. A below-limit value today may exceed
limits as history grows; the r2 charge is not a size invariant for all stores.

* Cost of captures is distinct from latent effects
S: [[file:../../priv/boot/core.bl::(defmacro lazy-seq][lazy-seq]] creates a thunk; =mapcat= maps then concatenates; [[file:../../priv/boot/rt.bl::(defn concat][concat]] is lazy.
An effect-free function/sequence can retain a whole world in its environment and
cost heavily to create, convey, inspect or force while returning only one deadline.
The initial summary discussion covered latent effects, not a bound on capture size.
A sequence can be finite yet large; realizing its final answer does not establish
bounded intermediate allocation or prove that all retained thunks were small.

Compare practical repairs before compiler features:
- Strict fold computes a scalar minimum/deadline from explicit relevant input,
  avoiding a retained concatenation graph. It may still scan every input: eager
  and pure do not mean bounded data work.
- A compact conversation-ID set narrows closure environment. It bounds retained
  context only when its population/byte size is itself bounded. Selecting all
  conversations in a hotel is smaller than all history but still grows.
- Page IDs plus admitted batched queries can bound both population and fan-out.
  A derived activity/deadline index may reduce scans, at maintenance/recovery cost.
- Generation-owned long-lived callbacks need lifecycle and code retention;
  that does not entitle them to capture raw stores or entire read worlds.

Proposed diagnostics should report binding/reference, capture site/escape/lifetime,
known eager versus deferred consumers, dependency identities, safe estimated charge
and unknowns. A compiler can conservatively identify free-variable retention and
suspicious world-capturing closures; it cannot infer exact native sharing, backend
work or final bytes in general. Runtime size observations may complement this but
must not realize caller continuations, dump secrets or recursively traverse an
unbounded capture graph. Use bounded inspection and cancellation budgets.
Do not conflate capture cost with system.linear's compiler-emission growth or
system.ownership's lifecycle contracts, and do not add a duplicate effect system.

* Optional memoization is not once-only execution
| Mechanism | Honest contract | Failure/concurrency/lifetime consequence |
|-----------+-----------------+------------------------------------------|
| Optional bounded cache (current path) | May reuse a pure result while admitted and present | Recompute on decline/eviction/race; correctness independent of retention |
| Lexical eager binding per captured read | Same already-computed value passed within one read | No cross-read reuse; binding and downstream closures can still retain broad data |
| Single-flight per key/revision (possible separate service) | Coalesce concurrent eligible computation attempts | Must specify waiter cancellation, owner death, error fan-out, recursion and capacity; does not guarantee lifetime once-only |
| Owned prepared read model | Readiness requires a specific prepared resource/view | Failure/unavailable/backpressure explicit, memory reservation and generation/drain required; no longer optional cache |
| Durable command/operation receipt | Original attempt identity controls effect admission/lookup | Must use journal/native/provider rules; a disposable cache is never its authority |

Recommend documentation saying "may reuse an admitted retained value" for this
optional cache, and "share the captured result within this read" for lexical
reuse. Once-only needs an explicitly scoped object/lifetime/protocol, including
failure semantics; never infer it from memo-by-basis's name or basis equality.
No documentation/code change to Beam Lisp is made here.

* Holistic alternatives revisited
A (ordinary values/APIs) gains the strongest immediate support: a local data/lifetime
repair restored useful reuse with existing APIs. Add bounded diagnostics before
inventing declarations. B (feature/capability values) can link read identity,
requested budgets and retention policy, but a =:cache true= field cannot certify
admission or cold-path cost. C (compiler roles/evidence) can check covered dependency
and capture boundaries and require explicit unknowns; it cannot prove a value fits
a real store's budget from a generic pure annotation. D (maintained data products)
can make preparation/availability explicit, but trades cache misses for admitted
maintenance/backfill/readiness obligations. None automatically wins from r2.

Keep the six concepts in RECOMMENDATION; refine read/evidence contracts with
retention and closure-cost dimensions rather than introduce a cache DSL or
universal "finite world" type. Canonical retained data, capture-local overlays,
local indexes, finite route models and durable business facts have different
lifetimes. Their composition should make those differences visible.

Bounded work remains open: the new repeated full/temporal timings do not establish
physical row/seek/byte bounds, query selectivity scaling, cache-disabled p95,
concurrent-viewer behavior, provider correctness or browser usability. Test the
cheap path AND deliberate cache unavailability, growing same-scope history and
new bases. A warm cache is an optimization, not the enforcement of G4.
