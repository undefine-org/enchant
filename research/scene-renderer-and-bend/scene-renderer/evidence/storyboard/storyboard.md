# Beam Lisp product film storyboard

## The story in one sentence

A creator writes “Hello, world.” and discovers that changing an idea can reshape
an entire living picture, without starting again.

**Working title:** Hello, world. **Master:** 60 seconds, 16:9, 60 fps.
**Audience:** people who make motion graphics, explain things visually, build
creative tools, or want programming to become a visual medium.
**Product identification:** Beam Lisp scene renderer — motion graphics written in
Lisp. **Viewer action:** make a first scene. A real getting-started destination
must exist before a release; do not invent a URL in the end card.

The emotional progression is delight → recognition → agency → confidence → desire.
The viewer first wants the picture, then sees themselves making it, then realizes
how much freedom its underlying rules give them. This is a product story rather
than a shader reel. Materials earn screen time by expressing what the creator
can change. The spectacle grows out of visible decisions.

The creative promise is **a world you can keep changing**. Demonstrate three
things: one change can carry through a composition; one definition can make many
expressive instances; the creator can address any moment precisely. Inspection
and export provide confidence underneath that promise. They do not become extra
feature announcements. The film requires no narrator and must make sense muted.
Sound amplifies cause and effect rather than explaining missing visual information.

[Open the visual storyboard](index.html). Its illustrations
are staging proposals, not captured product UI or finished film frames. The
60-second player is a timed sequence of storyboard poses, not the final motion.
The production gates below separate implemented primitives from proposed workflow.

## The visual contract

Follow the same coral-outlined picture, the same yellow seed, and the same
“Hello, world.” title throughout. The seed is a semantic object, not a cursor or
an unexplained mascot. Its small notch and one coral orbital line identify it
through materials and scale changes. Keep the original instance slightly larger
and near the viewer’s eyeline when a family appears. Hue alone must never carry
identity. No unrelated footage, alternate sample projects, stock devices, human
reaction shots, feature cards, or camera travel between presentation slides.

The studio is an instrument around the work: preview in the center, source or
properties at the left, time below, a restrained inspector at the right only when
needed. Never show all tools at once. The artwork owns at least 65% of a product
shot and the full frame during the payoff. Do not miniaturize a desktop interface
until it becomes decorative noise. A cursor enters once, performs an identifiable
action, then rests. It must never pretend to be the underlying cause of a
pre-rendered effect that the product cannot actually produce.

Use warm ivory for the working surface, near-black for text and chrome, yellow
for the persistent seed, coral for an authored change, and a thin cobalt accent
for selection or time position. Selected bounds have a corner notch and small
handles, so selection survives grayscale. Amber is reserved for diagnostics, not
brand emphasis. Do not add green success flashes. Changes resolve through stable
geometry, readable text and attached connections.

Typography has two jobs: an expressive serif belongs to the created picture;
a direct sans belongs to the instrument and product identity. Code uses a legible
monospace face and only a few actual lines. Brand, role labels and clocks do not
compete with the title. The final lockup uses the sans with generous negative
space. Texture stays on the artwork, never over interface text.

Camera movement establishes scale and relationships. Fast travel connects
readable moments; it does not replace them. Use overlap, anticipation, arcs and
controlled overshoot where physically appropriate. A fast move must have a
visible destination. Avoid identical easing on every property, elastic text,
unmotivated roll, generic fades, floating cards, and repeated pauses at every key.
Maintain spatial continuity through selection, bounding corners, the seed and
its connector. Editing, scrubbing and export are direct-manipulation moments:
the camera settles long enough for the causal action to be understood.

## Complete shot plan

### 01 — The thing you want to make · 00:00–00:04

**Viewer understands:** this makes beautiful motion, with a recognizable idea
inside it. **Feeling:** I want to make that.

Start close enough to see ink, a curved letter edge and the yellow seed’s woven
surface. In the first half-second, the seed passes through the counter of the
letter “o.” Follow its arc as it draws the coral connector taut. Pull back to a
confident composition: “Hello.” in large editorial type, one seed, three small
related marks, and material depth. The picture has purposeful rhythm, not a
background object spinning behind a slogan.

At 00:02.7 the camera begins a much deeper pullback. At 00:04 the picture is
visibly inside a preview window. The corner radius and timeline were outside
our initial crop; the world did not fade or change projects. Keep the seed near
58% across / 47% down during this reveal.

**Copy:** “Hello.” is artwork content. A small Beam Lisp identity becomes visible
with the workspace. **Sound:** a short tactile stroke, then a musical phrase
whose last note follows the seed. Start immediately; no logo sting pre-roll.
**Exit match:** the preview’s four corners become the studio frame in shot 02.
**Gate:** author and render this exact hero composition. Do not substitute the
old abstract ribbon film and call the opening product storytelling.

### 02 — Put the creator in control · 00:04–00:08

**Viewer understands:** this is a tool for making the picture, and it uses code.
**Feeling:** I can touch this.

The camera finishes its pullback into a deliberately sparse workspace. The
preview remains dominant. At the left, show the source of the selected title
and its text value, not a page of code. Below, one playhead is visibly connected
to the current frame. Beam Lisp and the category line are readable now, not only
at the end of the spot. Avoid fake desktop chrome and an operating-system dock.

The cursor selects “Hello.” in the source. A thin cobalt boundary identifies the
corresponding title in the picture. Move the cursor to the selected string and
pause for at least 0.6 seconds. Selection, the code highlight and the visible
word form one causal triangle. No floating line labeled “source of truth.”

**Copy:** “BEAM LISP” and “Motion graphics. Written in Lisp.” Small source content
and minimal real UI labels only. **Sound:** music makes room for the selection
click; no whoosh for the inspector. **Exit match:** the highlighted source string
and the title share the viewer’s eyeline into the edit.
**Gate:** polished filming workspace and source-to-object selection are proposed;
today’s postcard UI has form controls and an object selector, not this editor.

### 03 — Change your mind; the picture follows · 00:08–00:14

**Viewer understands:** words and layout belong together. **Feeling:** relief.

Replace the title string in one edit: “Hello.” → “Hello, world.” The scene first
shows the accepted new text, then its already-resolved layout in a short
presentation interpolation. The title’s measured extent grows; its frame widens;
the seed and connector keep their attachment. Neighboring elements make room.
Do not animate the letters squeezing to fit or imply a general constraint solver.

Let the final title and attached marks sit clearly for 1.3 seconds. A temporary
measurement line is allowed only if it helps the eye follow the change; it must
be visible in the inspector, not an invented magical trail. Keep the word that
changed and its geometric consequence within one fixation area. Do not cut
between a keyboard action and an unrelated beautiful result.

**Copy:** only the title before and after. No “responsive layout,” “smart,”
“automatic,” or “everything stays connected” headline. **Sound:** a quiet text
entry gesture, then a resolved chord when the new composition fits.
**Exit match:** the widened frame becomes the selected component boundary.
**Gate:** text measurement/layout/anchors exist; this hero’s exact dependent
layout and transition between accepted layouts must be authored and verified.
Source hot refresh must be implemented if the edit is filmed as live source.

### 04 — One decision has reach · 00:14–00:19

**Viewer understands:** the picture has a reusable definition. **Feeling:** agency.

Pull back slightly. Three instances of the selected component are already visible
as part of the artwork. In the source, highlight one material value. Change ivory
to coral. The three matching surfaces update together, with their seed placement,
content and local variation preserved. One thin selection rail connects the
source field to all three instance boundaries for the duration of the change.
The original keeps its identifying notch. Do not rely on the color switch alone.

Show the single definition clearly for 0.9 seconds after the update. No more than
five source lines occupy the active source region. Use the actual expression
from the authored project. Do not put an illustrative API in a fake editor.

**Copy:** no new headline. An authentic material value is product content.
**Sound:** one input click, one joined material sound, not three separate pops.
**Exit match:** those three instance boundaries become the near edge of a larger
family as the viewport widens. Nothing duplicates through an opacity pop.
**Gate:** reusable functions and explicit style values exist. The multi-instance
parameter edit and source/instance highlighting must work in this film project.

### 05 — Small program, expansive picture · 00:19–00:24

**Viewer understands:** one definition can produce many distinct things.
**Feeling:** possibility.

The source selection moves to the real mapping expression. Widen the camera to
reveal 24 keyed instances, already present outside the earlier crop. The original
remains near center, identifiable by its notch and scale. Differences are
intentional: titles, proportions and seeded surface detail vary. They are not
randomly colored copies. The source stays one short definition and one map call.

Carry the instances from a measured grid into an attached branching formation.
Connectors remain on their anchors. The original’s branch leads the eye from
source to the expanding system. The travel lasts under a second; the viewer
gets at least a second to see one rule beside its many results.

**Copy:** a real map expression, such as `(mapv world ideas)`, only after `world`
and `ideas` are defined in the project. No headline announcing “infinite.”
**Sound:** the original melody gains harmonics from the family. Avoid 24 clicks.
**Exit match:** the selected component remains fixed while its neighbors begin
a continuous geometric phrase.
**Gate:** map/functions, keyed identity and formation morphs exist separately;
compose them in the same project and retain a correspondence test.

### 06 — Give the idea a rhythm · 00:24–00:29

**Viewer understands:** the program describes motion as well as appearance.
**Feeling:** momentum.

The studio border recedes outside the crop as the camera travels into the
branching picture. The same instances flow into a ribbon, then a helix, with
parent/child relationships carried through. Their labeled faces turn as coherent
objects. The source is out of frame now; the work gets to be spectacular.

Use a short anticipation, a decisive sub-second uncoiling, and a longer arc that
carries velocity into shot 07. Avoid a three-pose slideshow. At the peak of the
move, keep the original seed on a readable trajectory; small orbital marks
provide relative scale. Real contour and attachment changes matter more than
camera roll. No line of copy describes the effect.

**Copy:** none. **Sound:** establish a clear rhythmic pulse tied to the phrase,
with one broad lift at the uncoiling. No continuous whoosh bed.
**Exit match:** the moving helix is precisely the frame that will be paused.
**Gate:** continuous scores and finite formation/contour morphs exist. This
composite project needs its own coherent scene and material evaluation.

### 07 — Time is under your hand · 00:29–00:34

**Viewer understands:** any moment can be visited directly. **Feeling:** control.

The camera exposes just the bottom timeline and a sliver of the studio. The
creator pauses on the helix. A clear playhead sits at a marked moment. Drag back
through the uncoiling; the scene returns along the same path. Jump to a distant
moment and back to the marked one. The exact same pose returns, including the
seed’s notch and connector crossing. The marked pose gets two readable holds,
at least 0.6 seconds each.

Make the pointer stop on the timeline before the camera moves again. The
playhead, marker and visible state must agree. Use the actual project’s sampled
timecodes. Do not create apparent speed by racing an unreadable clock, nor imply
stateful physics can be reversed without a declared model.

**Copy:** the timeline’s real time display only. No “deterministic” headline.
**Sound:** dip the music, use a subtle scrub texture while dragging, return the
same motif on the repeated pose. Keep time-jump audio deliberate, not accidental
backward playback of the whole score.
**Exit match:** the pointer leaves the playhead and selects the original seed.
**Gate:** independent time sampling exists; the new project must be seekable in
the review tool, with recorded equal-state/equal-frame evidence at the marker.

### 08 — The beauty still has structure · 00:34–00:39

**Viewer understands:** the creator can precisely understand and adjust the work.
**Feeling:** confidence.

Select the original. Its boundary, baseline and two actual anchors appear.
One compact inspector shows a typographic value and its resolved origin. Avoid
an encyclopedia of properties. The pointer selects the baseline anchor; the
connector endpoint highlights at that same location. Move the selected component

Show the anchor relationship for at least 0.8 seconds. Close the overlay through
the same selection action, not a scene cut. The chosen anchor becomes the pivot

size; artwork outlines retain their own material character.

**Copy:** real UI labels “Font” and “Anchor,” only if those controls exist. No
“understand everything” or source-trace claims. **Sound:** a short precise tick
for selection, a taut string sound for the attached move.
**Exit match:** zoom through the selected seed at the anchor’s exact location.
**Gate:** bounds/anchors and limited font/color origins exist. Spatial selection,
a draggable component and broader inspector UI are production work, not shipped
studio features. A numeric transform edit is an honest fallback.

### 09 — Keep the idea; change its voice · 00:39–00:46

**Viewer understands:** the same authored structure can have radically different
visual character. **Feeling:** creative freedom.

Dive to the selected seed’s material surface. Its notch, coral line and curvature

The changes come from one authored material control, briefly visible before

control demonstrations. Give the best metal frame a full second of clear viewing.
Avoid three full-frame crossfades between unrelated renders.

**Copy:** none. **Sound:** paper friction becomes weave and then a resonant metal
tone; the underlying melody remains the same.
**Exit match:** the metal edge aligns with the curved letter edge from shot 01.
**Gate:** paper/vector materials and the private GPU shader executor exist on

### 10 — This is the world you made · 00:46–00:51

**Exit match:** the exact preview frame is retained into the export action.

## Copy ledger and what carries the explanation

Primary advertising copy is deliberately limited to the brand, the five-word
category line and the four-word CTA. The category appears at the product reveal
and the close. The artwork has its own meaningful title; the UI and code show
only what the demonstrated task actually requires. Do not count tiny unreadable
labels as an excuse to add more text.

| Message | Convey it with | Do not add |
|---|---|---|
| This creates motion graphics | Finished opening, then the studio around it | “A revolutionary creative tool” |
| A text edit changes dependent layout | String edit, measured frame growth, retained attachment | “Intelligent responsive layout” |
| One definition makes a family | One highlighted rule beside its keyed instances | “Limitless scalability” |
| The scene is programmable | Authentic short definition and map call | Paragraph explaining Lisp |
| Time is directly addressable | Pause, marked pose, distant jump, exact return | “Deterministic temporal composition” |
| The work has inspectable structure | Selection, baseline, anchor and attached connector | “Total control” |
| Appearance can change deeply | Same silhouette/notch through paper, weave and metal | Names of shaders or renderer backends |
| The result is deliverable | Accepted preview → export → matching playable movie | Format icon clouds and “instant export” |
| The viewer should start creating | Final invitation and a real destination | Generic “learn more” or invented URL |

**Exact large-text inventory:** “Hello.”; “Hello, world.”; “BEAM LISP”; “Motion
graphics. Written in Lisp.”; “Make your first scene.” No other editorial headline.
**Small interface inventory:** only filmed controls and the actual source/value
snippet. “Font,” “Anchor,” “Export film” are proposed labels until implemented.
**Voiceover:** none in the master. Do not add narration to repair unclear blocking.

## Editorial rhythm and sound score

Build a score around actions, not a uniformly fast loop. Seconds 0–4 are tactile
and curious. The workspace reveal opens the stereo field. The accepted title
edit resolves a chord. Shared-rule and family shots add harmonics. The geometric
phrase introduces the main rhythmic drive. The scrub briefly strips it back.
Inspection is precise and quiet. Material travel grows from paper friction to
a resonant spectral texture. The final piece carries the widest arrangement.
Export suspends one note across an honest time cut; the final signature resolves
the opening motif. Important clicks remain audible on small speakers. No borrowed
recording, thundering bass as a substitute for articulation, or constant whooshes.

The duration budget is exact: 4 + 4 + 6 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 7 + 5 + 4 + 5 = 60.
Major readable islands: title after edit (1.3 s); rule beside family (≥1 s);
repeated timeline pose (≥0.6 s twice); anchor relationship (≥0.8 s); material hero
(≥1 s); finished title (1.4 s); complete close (3.8 s). These are minimum staging
allocations, not a claim that every audience will read at the same rate.

Fast moves can happen between those islands. Cut or simplify decorative movement
when it competes with the causal action. Headline text never rides a whip pan.
Use camera overshoot on the camera, not on measured type or a timeline playhead.
No strobing or repeated white flashes; achieve contrast through spatial framing,
material and musical dynamics.

## Production truth and dependencies

This is a storyboard for the intended product film, not evidence that the generic
studio already supports the finished sequence. The earlier GPU reel remains a
material test; it must not become the film by adding slogans over it.

| Film requirement | Existing foundation | Work before a truthful product capture |
|---|---|---|
| Measured title/frame/attachment edit | Pango outlines, layout bounds, anchors | Author hero constraints and accepted-layout interpolation |
| Live source edit and linked selection | BL source authoring; form UI; indexed objects | Coherent source refresh, source/object links, filming workspace |
| One rule and many instances | Functions, maps, scoped IDs, style values | Actual shared definition, captured controls and instance highlighting |
| Grid/branch/ribbon continuity | Keyed formations and explicit contour correspondence | Compose hero family and verify its paths/attachments |
| Exact timeline revisit | Arbitrary-time evaluation and seekable films | General composition preview with this project’s exact sampled marker |
| Useful selection/inspection | Object selector, bounds/anchor overlay, limited origins | Spatial picking or an honest selector/numeric edit; compact inspector |
| Paper/weave/metal in the same world | Vector materials; separate private GPU executor | Defined common scene/material boundary, identity and sampling policy |
| Export the accepted general project | Exact postcard revision export; offline builders | Admit/capture hero revision and validate matching canonical frames |
| Invitation to start | Checkout examples and documentation | Make the first-scene path obvious and supply the actual destination |

Do not let a polished mockup silently become a shipping claim. Product interaction
shots must be real captures or explicitly described as a concept in the release.
An offline rendered artifact may appear as artwork; it cannot masquerade as a
live parameter edit. Direct manipulation without implemented picking must use
the actual object selector or numeric field. Avoid accelerated preview claims:
retain actual response timing and use visible editorial time cuts when needed.

A practical production order is: author the hero and an edit-proof still; build
source/property/preview control for that one project; record the title, shared
rule, sampling and anchor actions; establish one supported material route;
render the continuous artwork choreography; capture the accepted export; then
edit the final spot. Review a silent gray-box animatic before spending time on
shader polish. No Blender-like general editor or compiler programme is required.

## Review gates before final production

1. After eight seconds muted, an unfamiliar viewer can identify a creative motion
   tool and point to what the creator is about to edit.
2. After fourteen seconds, the viewer can identify the input change and its
   consequence without reading a benefit headline.
3. At the family reveal, the source is visibly small and the outcomes visibly
   related; the original instance remains findable without relying on hue.
4. At the time demonstration, two returns to the marked moment match exactly;
   the pointer and clock never imply a different state from the picture.
5. The material sequence preserves the same shape/seed relationship. If it feels
   like a cut to a different render, redo the transition rather than label it.
6. The final picture visibly contains earlier decisions. If it could be replaced
   with any beautiful clip, the narrative has failed.
7. Export shows the accepted revision and the actual artifact. No performance or
   generic workflow claim is implied by a cosmetic progress animation.
8. The end answers “what is it?” and “what can I do next?” at phone viewing size.
   Sound can strengthen the story; turning it off cannot destroy the meaning.

Test these moments with unfamiliar viewers before final production.

## Thirty second adaptation

Do not simply play the 60-second master at twice the speed. Keep the same causal
spine and fewer demonstrations:

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 00–03 | Finished picture; pull back into the product |
| 03–09 | Title edit and measured layout/attachment response |
| 09–14 | One definition and its family; begin the geometric phrase |
| 14–18 | Pause, scrub, return to the same marked pose |
| 18–24 | Dive through material, pull back to the finished “Hello, world.” |
| 24–26 | Accepted export → matching delivered frame, with editorial ellipsis |
| 26–30 | Brand, category, first-scene invitation |

Drop the separate inspector beat and the standalone shared-color edit. Preserve
cause and effect, readable title, exact revisit, material continuity and the
identity of the project. A 15-second version would be a separate concept, not
this film squeezed until the product becomes illegible.
