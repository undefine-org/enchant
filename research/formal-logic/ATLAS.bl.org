* The big idea
#+begin_lede
Imagine writing a function and asking, in the same language: /what must be true before this runs, what can it return, and can this change break anything?/

The experiment gives Beam Lisp a common language of typed terms. Z3 and OxiZ can work on the same question. BL owns the meaning, and the answer brings its assumptions and limits with it.
#+end_lede

#+end_stats

#+begin_principles
Write the meaning once | Maps, arithmetic and outcomes keep the same meaning when we change the solver.
Make answers inspectable | Show the claim, its assumptions, a counterexample when available, and the scope of the result.
Use preview to improve the language | Fix incoherent behavior before programs have to depend on it.
Keep a clear next step | A language with precise semantics and checked proofs is a realistic direction. Every stage needs its own evidence.
#+end_principles

* Try the language ideas
A total solver array has an entry at every index. A BL map can have no entry at all. That small difference is why the language layer matters.

* Better language decisions
Preview gives us room to choose coherent rules. The goal is a language programmers can reason about without discovering exceptions by accident.

* Could Beam Lisp be fully formal?
Yes, if we choose a precise promise and build toward it in layers. There are several different ambitions hiding in that phrase.

#+begin_rosetta
Ambition | What it would mean for BL | What it does not buy automatically
Precise semantics | Every core construct has a mathematical account of values, outcomes and effects. | A proof that every program meets its author's intent.
Proof-aware programs | A module can carry contracts, assumptions and checked evidence. | A decision procedure for every possible property.
A total core | Core functions terminate; recursion needs a structural argument or another checked termination proof. | A ban on long-running actors in the effectful language around it.
A verified compiler | Compilation preserves the specified source behavior under an explicit target/runtime model. | Verification of OTP, the OS, hardware and every foreign library.
A fully verified application | Its stated end-to-end properties follow from checked component proofs and explicit environment assumptions. | Proof that its requirements are complete or the real world obeys those assumptions.
#+end_rosetta

** The direction I would choose
A small pure core, a rich effectful language around it, and evidence that travels with the code. Keep ordinary programming pleasant. Make a stronger claim require a stronger, visible justification.

#+begin_principles
Specify the core | Values, equality, evaluation order, exceptions and the meaning of each numeric/text operation belong in a precise semantics.
Distinguish partial correctness and termination | “If this returns, its balance is nonnegative” differs from “this always returns.” Total correctness needs both.
Keep effects explicit | Host calls, state, actors, time and failures need contracts and trace semantics. A snapshot becomes a stated assumption.
Make proof search replaceable | Solvers and tactics can propose evidence. A small checker validates supported proof objects against the exact claim.
Carry the dependency ledger | Report the definitions, assumptions, axioms, profiles, artifacts and runtime contracts each theorem uses.
Preserve meaning through compilation | Prove a lowering pass or validate each translation; retain the source-to-ANF-to-target connection.
#+end_principles

** The important limits
General recursion makes automatic termination and arbitrary program correctness undecidable in general. We can choose decidable fragments, accept user-supplied invariants and termination arguments, or return unknown. “Formal” does not mean the machine can answer every question. See [[https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/Turing_Paper_1936.pdf][Turing's original computability result]] and [[https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Definitions/Recursive-Definitions/][Lean's explicit treatment of recursion and termination]].

Actors can run indefinitely by design. We can prove safety (“no invalid message is sent”) while leaving progress conditional on scheduler fairness, delivery and failure assumptions. Cancellation, timeouts and hot upgrades need their own observable trace semantics.

Foreign calls should expose explicit contracts and admission policies. Treating an unmodeled host function as a pure mathematical function would hide exactly the uncertainty the interface is meant to reveal.

A checked proof can still establish the wrong specification. We need understandable contracts, executable examples and witnesses to help people review whether the claim is the one they intended.

#+begin_honest :title "A total core inside an effectful language"
I recommend exploring a total, pure core with explicit effects around it. Keep a supported general-programming path for actors and interop, with its assumptions visible. That gives BL a strong foundation without pretending the entire BEAM and outside world are already verified.
#+end_honest

** What this could feel like
#+begin_quote
“This refactor preserves the result, the failure behavior and the declared effects. Here are the assumptions it uses. This module still relies on an unverified host contract.”
#+end_quote

That is a useful language feature: a concrete, inspectable statement attached to the program. A proposed declaration might combine a precondition, a result contract, an effect signature and a termination claim. The exact surface syntax should follow the semantics, rather than deciding the semantics accidentally.

#+begin_decision :id formal-total-core-direction :title "Explore a total pure core and explicit effects" :status proposed
- [x] Specify a small core, allow stronger contracts and proofs, and make effect/runtime assumptions explicit.
- [ ] Require every ordinary BL program to carry a total-correctness proof immediately.
- [ ] Treat an SMT verdict alone as a complete formal foundation.

This is a recommended research direction. It does not change BL's language semantics or activate a new compiler gate.
#+end_decision

Primary reference points: [[https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Elaboration-and-Compilation/][Lean separates elaboration from a small trusted kernel]], [[https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Axioms/][Lean exposes axioms and additional trust dependencies]], and [[https://compcert.org/man/manual001.html][CompCert states an explicit behavior-preservation guarantee]]. These illustrate architectural principles, not proofs about BL. The design here is our proposed application of those principles.

** What native means for Beam Lisp
The common interface gives Beam Lisp ownership of its symbolic vocabulary, language outcomes, sharing, source maps and evidence. Both native workers can consume that vocabulary: Z3 has native C AST constructors and OxiZ has Rust term APIs. Both still need an adapter from BL data and an owned native lifetime. Native ASTs remain private to the worker while the immutable BL question can travel, be inspected, cached and replayed.

This architecture makes finite maps, tagged outcomes, coverage, permission relations, induction and synthesis reusable language features above either engine. Backend differences remain concrete questions about supported constructors, solver strength, observations, cores and retained artifact behavior. An OxiZ source module or a Z3 theory name alone establishes none of those results; the two corpora below provide the comparison at explicit scopes. Performance and memory advantages require their own measured workload evidence.

** What the exact runtime taught us
#+begin_honest :title "A concrete equality defect, with a small proposed fix"
The pinned SDK returns [true true false] for [(= 1.5M 1.5) (= 1.5 1.50M) (= 1.5M 1.50M)]. This violates transitivity. The cause is the scale-sensitive two-Decimal exception inside an otherwise exact-rational numeric family. The proposed fix removes that exception and exposes representation or quantization equality explicitly. Production runtime source has not been changed by this experiment.

Cross-list/vector value equality is a separate design choice: it can remain useful even when conj and type-sensitive operations distinguish the representations. Compiler rewrites must use a relation that respects the observable context.
#+end_honest

Advanced value prototypes add bit-field layouts, explicit byte order, pack/unpack lenses and wrapping/checked words. A total SMT Array has no finite extent or absence; a finite BL Map needs presence and extent descriptors. A Seq is not an effectful lazy stream. Finite sets, bags, maps and tagged sums are explicit language encodings above primitive terms, so their qualification is separate from native Set/Bag/recursive-datatype APIs. SMT FP equality, IEEE fp-eq and raw bit identity are also different relations: SMT has one NaN value, payload retention needs raw bits, and source-native BEAM floats exclude NaN and infinity.

#+begin_terms
