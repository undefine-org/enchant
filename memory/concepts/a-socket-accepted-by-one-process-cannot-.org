** ITEM A socket accepted by one process cannot be TLS-upgraded by another

Measured 2026-09-16 (OTP 29, a prototype CONNECT proxy written in beam-lisp).

A socket accepted by `gen_tcp:accept/1,2` is OWNED by the accepting process. `ssl:handshake/2,3` on such a socket (the TLS *upgrade* path, i.e. what a proxy does after replying 200 to `CONNECT`) fails if the caller is not the owner — and the failure is `{:error, {:dtls_upgrade, :notsup}}`, the same lie that masks an option error (ssl.erl wraps the whole thing in `catch error:{badmatch,_}`).

The fix is the ordering, not the API: transfer ownership BEFORE the handler touches the socket, and order the transfer with a message, because a spawn does not wait.

    p = spawn(fn -> (receive [:go sock] (handle sock))) end)
    :gen_tcp.controlling_process(s, p)     # the OWNER must make this call
    send(p, [:go s])                       # a bl vector literal; bl's receive matches it

Corollaries:
* Everything can stay `active: false`: passive `gen_tcp:recv`/`ssl:recv` may be called from ANY process, so the owner does not have to be the reader — only the upgrade needs the right owner.
* Non-owners may also `close/1`, and a handler that exits without closing leaks the socket until the listener dies.
* The same hand-off is already load-bearing in beam-lisp's Elixir gateway (`controlling_process` in `daemon/listener.ex`) — it is not a new discovery, it is the thing that must be ported rather than re-derived.
* And: bl's `receive` DOES match vector-tagged messages (`[:go x]`) sent with `erlang/send`, which is the primitive a bl accept loop needs for exactly this hand-off.
